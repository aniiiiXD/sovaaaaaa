Our starting thesis

Sell a measurable improvement to an existing workflow, with model adaptation and serving underneath. Start where a buyer already has traffic, data and a bill.

The companies you pointed to

Three routes to the same buyer.

These companies overlap. The distinction is where each puts its emphasis—not a ranking of their technology.

Dvar

Workflow-specific adaptation

Evaluate real tasks, select an open model, post-train it and serve it in India. Its banking example uses supervised training.

Lesson for us: start with a narrow task and a cost-per-outcome target.

Hyde

Enterprise ownership + delivery

Lesson for us: access to workflows and deployment expertise can be as important as training.

Lesson for us: sell quality, latency and cost together; measure the full system.

Who would pay us?

Start with a task owner.

Recommended entry: an AI product team with repeated, expensive work. The profiles below are customer hypotheses, not a list of existing customers.

Best first wedge • our recommendation

Their model bill grows with every customer.

AI software vendors doing support, document processing or search already have recurring tasks. Improve one step inside their product.

Who signs

CTO or VP Engineering; head of AI as the technical champion.

What they buy

A specialist model, evaluation suite, serving endpoint and ongoing improvement.

Why now

Inference cost is hurting margins, latency blocks adoption, or the current model fails a repeatable task.

What they must bring

Usable task examples, representative traffic, rights to use the data and a clear success metric.

Proof that wins the pilot: lower fully loaded cost per accepted task, at the agreed quality and p95 latency.
Qualify before training.Look for a recurring task, an accountable budget owner, usable evaluation data and enough volume to repay the engineering. A curious buyer with no workload is a research conversation.

Why would they switch?

Make the economics work.

A cheaper token is not the product. The customer needs savings on accepted work that repay setup, review and ongoing operation.

What we should sell first

One workflow. One paid proof.

A proposed commercial design for a small team. Pricing and timelines below need buyer validation; they are not competitor quotes.

A specialist-model pilot.

Start with support routing, document extraction or search planning. Pick the one where we can get data access and a committed buyer.

Price hypothesis: $15k–$40k for a tightly scoped 4–6 week pilot, after data access. A successful pilot can lead to an annual platform and improvement contract.

Sell against a quality, p95 latency and cost target. Do not promise a fixed accuracy lift before seeing the task.

1

Benchmark the existing system

Define acceptance with the buyer. Test prompting, retrieval and smaller models before deciding to train.

2

Adapt only what needs learning

Use SFT for demonstrations, preference methods for rankings, or RL where outcomes can be scored reliably.

3

Prove it on held-out work

Include failures, fallback and human review. Compare at matched quality and deployment constraints.

4

Deploy and keep improving

Serve with monitoring and rollback. Feed new failure cases into versioned training data.

Deprioritize: low-volume chatbots, buyers without data access or a budget owner, undefined “build us a model” projects, and frontier labs unless we are deliberately selling data or environments.

What still needs customer evidence?

The eligible buyer count, annual contract value, delivery effort, renewal rate and fully loaded savings. No customer pipeline, audited vendor margins or standalone custom-model TAM has been established here. The year-three capacity scenario is an annual revenue level, not cumulative sales.

How should we defend this against alternatives?

Compare against a better prompt, retrieval, a cheaper API, an existing fine-tuning platform and an internal ML team. Our proposed advantage must be repeatable evaluation, domain data access, delivery speed or deployment economics. Access to an open model alone is not enough.
